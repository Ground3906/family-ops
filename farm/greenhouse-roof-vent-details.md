# Greenhouse Roof and Vent Construction Details

**Locked 2026-08-18. Specifications only - not yet drawn as dimensioned sheets.** Read alongside `farm/greenhouse-design-sheet.md`, Roof and Ventilation sections, which these details expand on.

**Orientation convention for all cross-cut sections:** north/south wall sections drawn north-left, south-right. East/west wall sections drawn west-left, east-right.

---

## R-1 - Sheathing Termination and Metal Step at the Seam

**The single most consequence-bearing detail in the roof.** Sets where the Phase 1 deck ends - which is also exactly where the Phase 2 poly's north edge lands. Get this line wrong and Phase 2 means cutting sheathing on a standing, occupied roof instead of stripping a clean tear line.

**Assembly, north to south:**

| | |
|---|---|
| Sheathing | North section only, terminates at 9 ft 7-1/4 in up the rake from the south framing face |
| Blocking | Continuous, solid, between rafters, directly under the sheathing edge - gives the edge a fastening line and backs the step |
| Upper metal panel | Bears on sheathing, laps south across the sheathing edge and onto the lower panel |
| Step | 7/16 in (sheathing thickness), located INSIDE the 20 in lap zone - the lap is fully decked, so the step never shows as a bare transition |
| Lower metal panel | Bears direct on bare rafters south of the sheathing edge, ramps up 7/16 in to lie flat on the deck within the lap |
| Closure | Foam or butyl bead at the step line, inside the lap, sealing the void created by the ramp |

**Why the sheathing stops exactly here and not further south:** the edge was set at the upper panel's own south terminus - since that zone is already shaded by the lap, sheathing to that point costs nothing extra and gives Phase 2 a clean, pre-defined tear line with zero cutting.

**Ryder's note:** do not deck past this line. It is not a convenience stopping point - it is the design boundary between "permanent roof" and "Phase 2 removal zone."

## R-2 - Panel Layout and Chalk Line

**Order:**

| | |
|---|---|
| Stock | 12 ft (144 in), 28 panels total |
| Coverage assumption | 36 in net per panel - CONFIRM against actual Pro-Panel coverage width before ordering |
| Runs | 14 (covering the 40 ft building length), 2 panels per run |

**Cut:**

| Panel | Length | Cut |
|---|---|---|
| Lower (14 required) | 12 ft 0 in | None - full stock length |
| Upper (14 required) | 10 ft 10-1/4 in | Cut from 12 ft stock, 1 ft 1-3/4 in offcut each |

**Install - one chalk line per run, snapped from the top (easier to pull downhill than up):**

**Chalk line: 8 ft 4-1/4 in, measured DOWN the rake from the north framing face.** This is the lower panel's TOP edge. Lay the lower panel first, top edge on the line. The 8 in south overhang and the ramp onto bare rafters fall out automatically once the top edge is placed correctly - no second measurement needed at the south end.

**Upper panel placement:** bottom edge sits 20 in below the lower panel's top edge (i.e., 20 in below the chalk line). Lay the upper panel second, on top of the lower.

**Edge conditions, both fixed regardless of lap position:**
- North cantilever past finished wall surface: 4 in
- South overhang past finished wall surface: 8 in

**Why this sequence:** the upper panel's south edge is a controlled dimension - it is both the Phase 1 lap line and the future Phase 2 metal-to-poly seam. Referencing everything off the single chalk line (rather than measuring the upper panel's position independently) keeps that critical edge accurate without adding a second snapped line.

## R-3 - Metal-to-Metal Endlap (Phase 1)

**Location:** the 20 in lap between the upper and lower panels, centered over the R-1 sheathing edge and blocking.

**Assembly:**

| | |
|---|---|
| Substrate | Solid sheathing, full width of the lap zone (see R-1 - sheathing runs to the upper panel's south edge, so the entire lap sits on deck) |
| Seal | Butyl tape, full width, between the panels across the lap |
| Fasteners | Stitch screws through both panels into the blocking below, spaced per standard metal-roofing endlap practice |
| Rib condition | Ribs nest - panels align so no cross-panel rib mismatch at the lap |

**Phase 2 removal:** back out the stitch screws at this lap, the lower panel lifts free without disturbing the upper panel, which stays permanently in place.

## R-4 - Roof Edge Conditions (North and South, Shared Sheet, Two Sections)

Both sections drawn north-left/south-right per the standing orientation convention. Placed side by side on one sheet so both wall-head conditions read together.

### North section - riser wall head

**Assembly, low to high:**

| | |
|---|---|
| Riser framing | 2x6 studs, raked/beveled cap plate at 4.5:12 |
| Rafter bearing | Full-width on the raked cap, no notch, plumb-cut flush at the riser's outside framing face |
| Sheathing | Runs up over the rafter's plumb-cut top edge - no gap at the rafter line |
| Wall cladding | Pro-Panel, top course rib-ripped flat (field cut - coverage width of this course is nonstandard, flag for Ryder) |
| Drip edge | Standard profile, hemmed leg, set over the flat-ripped top course |
| Drip edge termination | **Turns down over the riser wall face** - covers the sheathing edge and the top of the flat wall course (locked over the alternative of stopping flush at the panel edge) |
| Roof panel | 4 in cantilever past the finished wall surface, laps over the drip edge |
| Closures | Outside closure strips under the roof panel's ribs at this edge - required here because this is the up-slope, windward edge; wind-driven rain can push under open ribs |

**Assembly order, bottom to top:** flat wall course, then drip edge over it turned down, then roof panel over the drip edge, with closures under the ribs.

### South section - beam/eave wall head

**Assembly, low to high:**

| | |
|---|---|
| Beam | 6x10 rough-sawn, birdsmouth-notched rafter bearing |
| Rafter above notch | 9-1/8 in remaining depth |
| Sheathing | Runs up over the rafter's remaining depth and plumb-cut south face |
| Wall cladding | Pro-Panel, top course rib-ripped flat, same field-cut condition as the north |
| Drip edge | Standard profile, hemmed leg, over the flat-ripped top course |
| Roof panel | 8 in overhang (Phase 1 metal) past the finished wall surface, laps over the drip edge |
| Closures | Not required - this is the down-slope, shedding edge; water is leaving here, not being driven in |

**Assembly order, bottom to top:** flat wall course, then drip edge over it, then roof panel over the drip edge. Water sheds off the roof, breaks clean at the drip edge's hemmed leg, falls clear of the wall face - nothing runs behind the cladding.

**Wall buildup, both sections, identical:** approx 7/16 in OSB sheathing + approx 1/4 in flat Pro-Panel = approx 3/4 in outboard of the framing face. This buildup is why the finished-surface overhang measurements (4 in north, 8 in south) differ slightly from framing-face measurements - always measure and build to the finished surface.

## R-5 - Poly Eave Termination (Phase 2 only)

**Not built in Phase 1.** Applies when the poly is installed at conversion.

| | |
|---|---|
| Overhang | 4 in past the finished wall surface |
| Termination | Breather tape only, full panel width, over the cut flute ends |
| **NOT used** | U-profile, snap-fit channel, or any clamped edge trim |

**Why no mechanical edge:** at 4.5:12, a heated poly roof sheds - that's the pitch's job. A snap-fit or clamped channel at the leading edge of a shedding surface is a catch point for sliding snow/ice, and the slab takes the channel (and the tape under it) with it when it lets go. A flush-taped edge has nothing to grab.

**Maintenance implication, accepted:** the tape is a wear item and will need periodic reapplication - an accepted tradeoff against losing hardware to snow slide. Since this is a Phase 2 detail years out, a mechanical edge can be reconsidered at that time without any Phase 1 rework.

## V-1 - Vent Panel Construction (both riser exhaust and south intake locations)

**Applies to all 16 panels** (8 riser exhaust at 4 ft x 24 in, 8 south intake at 4 ft x 27 in).

| Layer | Spec |
|---|---|
| Skin | Pro-Panel, ribs matched and aligned to the wall's own panel profile |
| Overlap | 1/2 in lap on all four edges - panel nests into the surrounding wall's rib pattern, using the panel's own profile geometry as the primary weather seal |
| Core | 2 in rigid polyiso, foam-safe adhesive only, **inset from the panel edges** - must stop short of the 1/2 in lap zone or the metal-to-metal overlap cannot physically close |
| Seal | Thin rubber strip, mounted on the FIXED WALL-SIDE FRAME - never on the moving panel, which would abrade the seal every open/close cycle |
| Perimeter gaskets | Not used - owner's call, accepted with the infiltration tradeoff noted below |
| Material source | Additional Pro-Panel siding sheets ordered specifically for cutting these 16 panels, matching the wall's rib profile |
| Hinge | Continuous piano hinge, top-mounted, outswing (awning-style) - north panels rack to the underside of the riser's doubled cap plate; south panels rack to the underside of the sill plate |
| Cut method | Nibbler or shears only - never an abrasive blade |

**Weight, this construction:** approximately 1.5 lb/sq ft. South panel (9 sq ft): ~14 lbs. North panel (8 sq ft): ~12 lbs. Both intended to sit comfortably within a single wax cylinder actuator's typical lift range (~20-30 lb) - **actuator spec still unverified against this figure, see design sheet Open Items.**

**Infiltration tradeoff, accepted:** ungasketed panels are a real air-leak path, most costly at the north (exhaust) panels where warm stack air collects at the peak. Owner's explicit tradeoff for lighter, simpler actuation rather than fighting gasket-seal-breaking force on every cycle.
