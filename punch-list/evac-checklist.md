## 4. Human supply kit (per-person backpacks, x8)

- N95 mask + goggles per person — wildfire smoke protection
- Water: 3 gal/person target (household total ~24 gal, split across bags/vehicles)
- 3-day food supply — see Chow Hall list below
- Protective clothing: sturdy boots, long pants, cotton long-sleeve (avoid synthetics)
- Hygiene: toilet paper, wet wipes, hand sanitizer, heavy-duty trash bags
- Tools/comm: NOAA weather radio, flashlight/headlamp per person + batteries, whistle, paper county map
- Phone charger / battery pack

### Chow Hall — emergency food, no stove/no fridge required

- Tuna/chicken pouches (pull-tab, not cans)
- Peanut butter + individual cracker packs
- Shelf-stable milk boxes
- Beef jerky / meat sticks
- Canned beans, pull-tab
- Applesauce pouches
- Granola bars, fruit snacks, string cheese (short shelf life — rotate)
- Trail mix, dried fruit
- Nut butter packets, protein bars
- Instant oatmeal packets — only if hot water is realistic
- Canned food (soup, chili, vegetables) — dedicated can opener packed with it
- Baby: ready-to-feed formula (no mixing) once he's here; pouches once on solids
- Rule of thumb otherwise: if it needs a stove or fridge, it doesn't go in the bag — canned food above is the deliberate exception, opener travels with it

## 9. Last-minute home defense (only if time permits)

If the order is immediate, skip this section entirely and leave.

- Close and lock all windows; close interior doors to slow fire spread
- Leave porch and indoor lights ON so firefighters can see the house through smoke
- Pull curtains and fabric furniture away from windows, toward room center
- Turn off the main gas valve / close propane tanks
- Move plastic patio furniture, toys, doormats off wooden decks, bring indoors
- Park vehicles in the driveway facing outward, windows up, keys in ignition
