# Floor Buyer Brief — 2027 Sale Cycle

For the Sale Committee's Request for Proposals process (Sale Committee Guidelines §3). Written ahead of the committee's own printed cycle (interest letters by end of March, RFP by June) so the terminal-sale changes below are reflected in this year's floor-buyer relationship from the start, rather than discovered mid-cycle.

**Status note:** the structure described below is proposed for the Fair Book and has not yet been adopted by the Fair Board (see `fair/fairbook/delta-log.md`, entries SALE-TERMINAL-01 and POULTRY-01). It is circulated to the committee now because the RFP cycle runs ahead of the board's own walkthrough schedule, and floor buyers need accurate expectations before they bid, not after. If the proposal changes at the walkthrough, this brief will be updated and recirculated.

## What is changing for floor buyers

Floor buyers are buyers of processed product. They are not, and under the proposed structure cannot be, buyers of a live animal. This is the one line every other change in this brief follows from.

## The route

1. **No Saturday-night pickup.** Under current practice, floor buyers already leave purchased animals on the grounds through the sale evening rather than hauling Saturday night. That practice becomes the printed rule under the proposal: every sale animal, floor-bought or premium-bought, stays in the fair's custody until delivery.
2. **Fair-controlled delivery, the morning after the sale.** The Fair Board, not the buyer and not the exhibitor's family, delivers every sale animal to a designated slaughter-processing facility. A floor buyer does not send its own truck to collect a live animal from the grounds.
3. **Product pickup at the facility.** A floor buyer's purchase is fulfilled by picking up product at the designated facility, per whatever arrangement the buyer has with that facility, not by taking possession of a live animal at any point.

## Designated facilities — two lanes

The proposal has the Fair Board designate, each year, one or more facilities holding a valid USDA inspection for animals whose product is intended for resale, and separately allows the Board to designate custom-exempt facilities for purchasers taking product for their own use. Floor buyers, who move product onward, are the inspected-lane's primary customer. The committee's RFP should confirm which lane(s) a bidding buyer needs and whether the buyer has an existing relationship with an appropriate facility or needs the fair to arrange one.

## Floor price's expanded role

Floor price currently applies to the exhibitor's private-sale option. Under the proposal it also becomes the backstop price for two situations that did not exist in the current book:

- **Undeclared animals.** An animal not declared for sale by the printed deadline may be consigned at floor price rather than through the auction ring.
- **Illness, injury, or death.** A veterinarian-certified sale animal that cannot go through the ring is purchased by the sale committee at floor price, so the exhibitor still receives a check.

Both situations mean floor-priced volume in a given year could run higher than in past years, independent of how many families choose the private-sale option. The committee should account for this when negotiating floor-buyer capacity, since it is now load-bearing for the sale's ability to guarantee every consigned animal a check, not just a convenience for buyers.

## Market poultry entering the pool

If the proposed Market Poultry section is adopted, chicken pens and turkeys become sellable, terminal market lots for the first time, subject to the same custody and delivery structure as every other species. Volume is expected to be small in the first years (turkeys in particular have no current county exhibitors; this section exists partly to recruit them), but the committee's facility and floor-price planning should have a placeholder for poultry from the first RFP cycle rather than treating it as a later addition.

## Rest before slaughter

Some buyers, historically mostly beef buyers, have asked for a purchased animal to rest for a period before slaughter to improve meat quality. Under the current book this happened through a live release to the exhibitor's family, which the terminal-sale proposal ends for any reason, without exception. The proposal instead has the Fair Board arrange any requested rest period inside the designated-facility chain, holding cost withheld from sale proceeds like any other delivery cost. Floor buyers who are themselves processors already hold purchased cattle in their own yards as a normal part of business; the RFP should ask each bidder directly about holding capacity and typical time-to-kill, so the committee can set realistic rest-period expectations and pricing before the season starts rather than negotiating it animal by animal.

## Questions for the RFP

- What is your holding capacity, and what is your typical time from delivery to kill?
- Do you hold a current USDA inspection, and can you also handle custom-exempt product for buyers taking product for personal use, or only one lane?
- Can you accommodate a purchaser-requested rest period before slaughter, and what does that cost?
- What is your capacity across species, including a small first-year volume of market poultry?
