# Sheep/Goat Barn — Layout Optimization Brief
## Custer County Fairgrounds | Design Handoff

**Deliverable:** 5 distinct layout options, visual-first.
**Baseline reference:** `fair/maps/Sheep_Goat_Barn_Map_REV6.png`

---

## 1. The Problem

The 2026 sheep/goat barn layout failed on three fronts:

- **Electrical:** outlets are exterior-wall only. Extension cords ran across animal cages to reach interior areas. Tripping hazard, animal welfare concern, and the wire cages were parked directly in front of the electrical panel, which tripped breakers with no safe access to reset them.
- **Wasted space:** the pen arrangement left significant floor unused.
- **Overflow:** swine holding pens were relocated outside during the swine show because there was no room inside. Outdoor conditions were too warm to be workable.

The current layout is a default, not a design. This effort replaces it.

---

## 2. The Building

**Exterior:** 40' x 48' pole barn (2x6 walls)
**Interior clear space:** ~39' x 47'
**Floor:** concrete
**Construction:** metal siding and roof, no insulation
**Attached:** Beef Barn on the west side

---

## 3. Hard Constraints — Do Not Violate

### 3.1 Structural / Immovable

| Element | Spec | Location |
|---|---|---|
| Large livestock scale | 4' x 10' | West side, **FIXED, cannot be moved** |
| Opening #1 (no door) | 4' wide | West wall, 8'-12' from NW corner → Beef Barn |
| Opening #2 (no door) | 4' wide | West wall, 8'-12' from SW corner → Beef Barn |
| Garage door | 9' x 8' | East wall, centered (15.5' to each edge). Faces Show Ring, 10' gap. Swine exit on show day. |
| South man door | ~3' | South wall, shifted east to clear cages. Swine entry on show day. |
| Electrical panel | See 3.2 | North wall, 5' from NW corner |

### 3.2 Electrical Panel Clearance — Safety Critical

The panel sits on the **north wall, 5 feet from the NW corner.**

**Required clear zone: 36" deep x 30" wide, floor to ceiling.** This is standard electrical working-space clearance and it is not negotiable.

- **Wire cages are prohibited** anywhere in or in front of this zone. This is what failed in 2026.
- **Animal pens may sit adjacent** but not within the clear zone. Pen bar stock sits low enough that the panel remains visible and reachable.
- Nothing may be stored, stacked, or staged in this zone at any point during fair week.

### 3.3 Placed Once, Then Effectively Fixed

**Swamp cooler — 2.5' x 4', oriented N-S.**

Technically portable, but once filled with water it is too heavy to relocate mid-fair. It gets **one placement decision made before fair opens**, and that placement must work for all eight phases. Treat it as harder than fixed: a fixed object at least tells you where it is.

### 3.4 Pens and Cages — Stationary Once Set

**This is a critical design constraint.**

The 20 animal pens and 4 wire cages are placed at setup and **do not move for the duration of the fair.** Once shavings, feed, tack, and exhibitor gear are in a pen, it is furniture, not equipment. Relocating them mid-fair is a last resort, not a design option.

**Consequence for the design team:** every layout must function as a **single static arrangement** that absorbs all eight phases. The empty floor adapts. The penning does not.

## 4. Inventory

### 4.1 Current Equipment

| Item | Qty | Nominal Dims | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Animal pens | 20 | 4' x 6' | Modular interlocking. 4' panels = backs/gates. 6' panels = shared dividers. 1" bar stock. |
| Wire cages | 4 | 6' x 6' | 1" bar stock. **Relocatable** — not locked to corners. Currently 2 NW, 2 SW. |
| Fitting stands | 6 | ~4' x 20" | Grooming/fitting stands. Genuinely mobile, cluster where kids work. |
| Large scale | 1 | 4' x 10' | **FIXED** |
| Small scale ("Paul") | 1 | 3' x 5' | Weigh-in position required at fair start, relocatable afterward |
| Swamp cooler | 1 | 2.5' x 4' | See 3.3 |

### 4.2 Dimensional Tolerance — Mandatory

**Drawings lie. Design to real numbers.**

All dimensions above are **nominal**. Actual footprint runs larger because:
- 1" bar stock adds to outside dimensions on both axes
- Pin-and-sleeve joints add width at every connection point

**Rule: add 3" per axis to every pen and cage.**

- A 4' x 6' pen occupies **4'3" x 6'3"** of floor
- A 6' x 6' cage occupies **6'3" x 6'3"** of floor

Design to **outside dimensions, never centerlines.** Across a run of five pens this is 15" of drift — enough to discover a wall in the way on setup day.

---

## 5. Circulation Rules

### 5.1 Aisles
**Minimum aisle width: 4'.**

### 5.2 Gate Orientation — Safety Rule
**Every gate must swing toward a wall or a blocking surface.** An open gate must never create an escape lane for an animal. This constrains gate orientation on every pen in every option.

### 5.3 Perimeter Walkways — Strongly Favored
Walkways against exterior walls are preferred, and the rationale should be visible in the design: **exterior walls are where the outlets and the electrical panel live.** Perimeter access is functional, not aesthetic.

---

## 6. Phase Sequence

The barn serves multiple functions across fair week. **Pens stay put through all of it.** Only the mobile elements change.

| Phase | Window | Configuration |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Pre-Tue 3pm | Setup. Pens and cages placed. Swamp cooler placed and filled — locked from here. |
| 1 | Tue Jul 14, 3-6pm | **Weigh-in hub.** All species. Both scales running in parallel — one species on the large scale, two on the small. Beef loop runs through both west openings. |
| 2 | Tue 6pm – Wed 2pm | All animals in place. Housing configuration. |
| 3 | **Wed Jul 15, 3-8pm** | **Swine show support.** Holding pens brought in (1500), chute set to entry gate. South man door = entry, garage door = exit. Show runs 6-8pm. |
| 4 | Wed 8pm – Thu 9am | **Teardown.** Holding pens and chute out. Back to housing plus walkways. |
| 5 | **Thu Jul 16, 9am-2pm** | **Sheep show (9-11am), Goat show (12-2pm).** The barn's primary purpose. |
| 6 | Thu 2pm – Sat | Housing. |
| 7 | Sat Jul 18, 1-4:30pm | Livestock auction support. |

### 6.1 The Tight Turn
**Phase 3 → 4 → 5.** Swine show ends 8pm Wednesday. Sheep show starts 9am Thursday. That teardown happens at night with a tired crew, and it has to land clean. Layouts that make this transition simple score higher.

### 6.2 What Actually Moves Between Phases
Only these four things:
1. Swine holding pens (in Wed 3pm, out Wed night)
2. Fitting stands (6, as needed)
3. Small scale (weigh-in position, then relocatable)
4. Chute to entry gate (Phase 3 only)

---

## 7. Fitting Stand Zone

Plan space for **6 fitting stands.**

**Requirement:** the fitting zone doubles as the **swine holding pen footprint during Phase 3.** Sheep exhibitors are not fitting during the Wednesday evening swine show, so the space is available. This means holding pens do not have to displace anything else.

**Stretch goal:** if a layout can support a *permanent* dedicated fitting zone without costing pen count or aisle width, that scores higher. Dual-use is the realistic expectation, not a compromise.

---

## 8. Future-State Overlay — Required on Every Option

Each option must show three tiers visually distinct from one another:

| Tier | Representation | Content |
|---|---|---|
| **Current** | Solid | 20 pens, 4 cages — existing inventory |
| **Proposed purchase** | Dotted or highlighted | **4 additional 4' x 6' pens**, placed as dedicated swine holding pens |
| **Expansion ceiling** | Distinct third treatment | Maximum additional pens this layout could absorb beyond the proposed 4, **with the count stated numerically** |

**Why this matters:** this is procurement ammunition. "This layout supports 8 more pens" is a fundamentally different board conversation than "we'd like to buy some more pens." The visual makes the case; the number closes it.

---

## 9. Design Direction

**Nothing conventional is required. Nothing is off the table.**

The current perimeter-pen arrangement is a default that has never been challenged. Explore aggressively:

- Back-to-back pen rows
- Pods and clusters
- U-shape and horseshoe configurations
- Island arrangements
- Angled or diagonal runs
- Anything else the geometry supports

**The constraints in Sections 3, 4.2, and 5 are the guardrails. Within them, be creative.** The five options should be genuinely distinct approaches, not five variations on one idea.

**Primary goals:** maximize usable space, minimize animal and handler movement.

---

## 10. Deliverable Specification

**Five layout options. Visual-first.** Diagrams carry the information. Text supports only where a diagram cannot.

### Per option:
1. **Scaled layout diagram** — to scale, wall thickness accounted, all fixed elements shown, three-tier future-state overlay per Section 8
2. **Phase walkthrough** — visual sequence showing how the static layout absorbs each of the 8 phases, with mobile elements (holding pens, fitting stands, small scale, chute) shown in position
3. **Brief written notes** — only where a diagram cannot convey the point

### Across all five:
4. **Scored comparison matrix** on four axes:

| Axis | What it measures |
|---|---|
| **Space** | Usable floor maximized, dead space minimized |
| **Movement** | Animal and handler travel distance minimized across all phases |
| **Phase adaptability** | How cleanly the static layout absorbs holding pens, stands, and the Phase 3→4→5 turn |
| **Animal flow** | Quality of traffic paths — weigh-in loop, swine entry/exit, show-day movement |

---

## 11. Accessibility Note for Diagrams

**Do not rely on red-vs-green to carry meaning.** Use brightness, shape, position, labels, or colorblind-safe pairs (blue/orange, blue/yellow, purple/tan) for any distinction that matters — including the three-tier future-state overlay in Section 8.
