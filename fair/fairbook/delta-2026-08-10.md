## FB-AUTHORITY-02 — "the Authority" defined

Supersedes FB-AUTHORITY-01's undefined use.

- **Old Custer text:** none. Verified 8/10/2026: the term "the Authority" appears ZERO times in `draft.md`. FB-AUTHORITY-01 proposed the umbrella term but no definition was ever written, and the assembled book used the term anyway. This is audit Item 4's real defect: not positioning, absence.
- **New text (CC 1):** "Each person listed below is the Authority when acting within their own role. No person acts as the Authority outside it." Fair Board — all matters, final interpretation of these rules. Superintendent — their own department; lead for classes, weights, order, and running the show. Judge — the class being judged, while judging it. Licensed veterinarian — animal health, fitness, and slaughter readiness; may refuse or excuse an animal. CSU Extension agent and FFA advisor — membership, eligibility, enrollment records, and conduct.
- **CSF cite:** structural only. CSF's "Authority" is a single statutory body; Custer has no equivalent, so the term is built as a role-scoped collective.
- **Reason for DISCUSS:** Matt's formulation, 8/10/2026: "the authority is an analagous blob that morphs. The vet doesn't give written warning for coaching." Role-scoping is what makes that legible to a parent. The vet excuses an animal and never writes a coaching warning; the judge rules her ring and never rules on a health certificate. This is the neutral-enforcement principle stated positively for the first time: the disqualifying fact always comes from whoever is competent to generate it.
- **Enforced-by:** self-defining.

## FB-SUPER-01 — superintendent authority

- **Scope, per Matt's correction 8/10/2026:** ONLY rules where the current book vests a responsibility in the superintendent alone. Rules that already share the responsibility are untouched.
- **New text:** where the current book vests a responsibility in the superintendent alone, that responsibility restates as vested in the Authority, with the superintendent named as lead. No approval step is added; the superintendent simply runs it. The Authority never divests.
- **The nine sole-owner rules (full sweep, 8/10/2026):** (1) early release of exhibits, record books, ribbons — "at the discretion of the superintendent"; (2) hardship cases — "Superintendents have the authority to deal with hardship cases" (twice); (3) releasing animals from the grounds — "until released by the superintendents" (twice); (4) checkout before leaving — "must check with the superintendent or designee" (twice); (5) feeder animal early release — "at the discretion of the superintendent"; (6) breaking classes by weight — "at the discretion of the superintendent" (five times: market general, beef, swine, sheep, goat); (7) barn space assignment — "Space in the livestock and small animal barns will be assigned by the Superintendents"; (8) changing class order — "Superintendent has the right to change the class order as needed"; (9) refusing an exhibit — "The Superintendent reserves the right to refuse any exhibit deemed unsuitable for competition" (twice, exhibits and photographs).
- **Left untouched, already shared:** the health review committee, the discipline panel, protest handling, everything reading "superintendent or Fair Board", "Superintendent or Judge", and stall checkout with the project leader.
- **Reason for DISCUSS:** reverses an existing county assignment of responsibility, so it cannot ride in as cleanup. FLAGGED DRAMA-PRONE: a long-tenured superintendent will hear "we are taking your show away." The answer that works is that the rule changes nothing about who runs the show, only about who the show belongs to. Rule (7) is the stall-assignment flashpoint already logged in `notes.md`.
- **Cross-ref:** resolved by FB-AUTHORITY-02, which places the superintendent inside the Authority rather than above or below it, so "the Authority decides" and "the superintendent is lead" stop being in tension.

## CC 2.1 — Who May Exhibit

- **Old Custer text:** membership gate printed ONCE, market livestock only: "Exhibitors in the divisions of this department must be bona fide members of Custer County 4-H or Custer County FFA". Non-market, FCS, and showmanship never state their entry gate at all. Open Division carried a welded sentence doing three jobs: "Entry must be made by the exhibitor. Exhibitors can reside in a county other than Custer, but entry can only be entered in one county fair in the state, this is excluding the Colorado State Fair."
- **New text:** CC 2.1.1 — an exhibitor in any 4-H or FFA division must be a bona fide member of Custer County 4-H or Custer County FFA. Residence in Custer County is not required. CC 2.1.2 — an exhibitor in the Open Division is not required to hold 4-H or FFA membership and is not required to reside in Custer County. CC 2.1.3 — an exhibit entered at the Custer County Fair must not be entered at any other county fair in Colorado. The Colorado State Fair is excepted.
- **Reason for DISCUSS:** "Residence in Custer County is not required" appears nowhere in the current book. It states only what the membership rule already does, but saying it out loud will change behavior, because families who assumed they were shut out will learn they can enroll. Real effect even though the rule did not move. The Open Division sentence is split because it welded who-may-enter to where-an-exhibit-may-show, which is why the State Fair carve-out only ever appeared in one section instead of in front of every family.
- **Cross-ref:** closes GEN-01 (residency). The gate is membership, not residence.

## CC 2.7 — Interference with Competition

Supersedes the coaching rule.

- **Old Custer text, printed verbatim EIGHT times:** "Coaching / cueing / whistling/ calling by parents from outside the show ring during the show is not allowed. Written warnings will be issued to the parent. After the third warning the 4-H or FFA member will be disqualified from the competition. Warnings are cumulative between species."
- **Source finding (full-source scan, 8/10/2026):** CSF has NO coaching rule. The word "coach" appears once in the entire 689-page handbook, in a horse list of who is bound by the GCR. Cueing, cuing, whistling: zero hits. IAFE has none either. The only near-hit in the whole stack is a horse rule, "No deliberate interference with the horse from outside the ring," which addresses spooking the animal. The state's answer is that the judge handles it, because showmanship scores independence.
- **New text:** adopts CSF GCR "Prohibition of Interference with Competitions" verbatim, noting IAFE Item 7 states the same prohibition. Custer Exceptions print the coaching conduct as EXAMPLES of interference with other exhibitors: (a) coaching, cueing, or directing an exhibitor from outside the ring during a class; (b) whistling, calling, shouting, or clapping to signal an exhibitor in the ring; (c) gesturing, making faces, or signaling to an exhibitor or to an animal in the ring; (d) striking, pounding, or banging on rails, panels, or gates; (e) any deliberate interference with an animal from outside the ring. Applies to every person on the grounds. Examples illustrate the rule and do not limit it.
- **The keystone, Matt's framing 8/10/2026:** the injured party is the other exhibitors in the ring, not the coached child. That is what makes it enforceable without proving anything about the coached kid, and it is why the rule anchors on interference rather than on own-work.
- **Ladder:** first violation written warning, second forfeits show premiums, third disqualifies the exhibitor from the show. Cumulative across all species, permanent per CC 2.8.1. A violation that also violates the IAFE Code forfeits premiums, awards, and auction proceeds.
- **Reason for DISCUSS:** the adoption is verbatim but the examples, the merged ladder, and perpetuity are Custer-authored. Resolves a defect nobody had caught: the book carried TWO three-strike ladders thirty lines apart with different triggers and different consequences — the coaching ladder above, and the own-work ladder ("one written warning ... second written warning will result in loss of show premiums ... third written warning will result in disqualification"). They could not both survive consolidation. Custer's ladder is kept; IAFE's forfeiture of premiums, awards, and auction proceeds is added on top, because sale money is the only consequence a repeat family actually feels.
- **Enforced-by:** the Authority, role-scoped per FB-AUTHORITY-02 — the judge inside a class she is judging, the Fair Board, superintendents, Extension, and the FFA advisor outside one. Matt's direction 8/10/2026: judges will not issue warnings in practice, so the Authority carries it, and the judge is the Authority only while judging. This closes the neutral-enforcement gap in the old rule, which said warnings "will be issued" and never named an actor — the one rule in the book that still had board members papering parents at the rail.
- **Political note:** Matt is the only board member who questions the coaching rule and expected to lose a straight repeal vote. The examples structure means no repeal is needed: the veterans' language survives in the book, printed as an illustration of an adopted standard rather than as county invention. The alignment argument stays intact.

## FB-IAFE-01 — adoption of the IAFE National Code of Show Ring Ethics

- **Old Custer text:** the county book references IAFE nowhere. It carries a four-line ethics rule: "No unethical practices will be allowed:" followed by doctoring/doping/injections for body fill-in; ice packs or refrigerants to alter finish; no altering color or structure of hair or wool except trimming and blocking; and "All market animals may be subject to random drug testing."
- **Coverage scan, Custer against IAFE's eight:** Item 5 (surgery, injection, applied substances) — Custer has a narrower version. Item 4 (drug residues, testing consent) — partial; Custer says testing may happen, never that entry is consent, never addresses lab results. Item 2 (health certificates) — yes, differently, via vet checks during fair and before sale. Items 1, 3, 6, 7, 8 — absent as printed rules. Roughly two and a half of eight.
- **Undeclared IAFE-derived practice already in the book:** tag stays with the animal all fair; certified scale calibrated yearly; vet check before sale; Meat Quality Assurance required; signed Wholesome Meat Act Disclosure; no breeding animal in market classes. Ownership integrity, instrument integrity, and food-chain integrity, with no source ever printed.
- **New text:** adopt the IAFE National Code of Show Ring Ethics by reference, as CSF does, with a signed form. Item-by-item dispositions locked 8/10/2026: Item 1 (ownership, length, age) ADOPT — mechanisms already exist in mouthing for age, brand inspection, possession forms, bills of sale, split between Extension and the fair. Item 2 (health certificates) ADOPT in the examples format. Item 3 (own care and grooming) ADOPT — Custer's existing own-work rules print underneath as examples. Item 4 (drug residues and testing consent) ADOPT WHOLE. Item 5 (surgery, injection, applied substances) ADOPT — Custer's four current ethics lines print underneath as examples. Items 6, 7, 8 ADOPT.
- **On Item 4 and the may-versus-shall question:** IAFE Item 4 creates authority, not obligation. It never says the fair must test anyone. FB-TEST-01's "may" survives adoption fully intact; it governs whether a sample is pulled, not whether the fair has the right to pull one. The line that does cost something is different: "all treatments involving drugs or medications for the sole purpose of protecting the animal's health shall be administered by a licensed veterinarian." That ends a parent treating their own animal in the barn. Adopted whole, deliberately — it is the neutral-enforcement principle applied to the barn, and the vet scope of work can carry it.
- **Signature:** exhibitor AND parent, per CC 2.8.2's agent-and-representative principle and the perpetuity direction. The form is Addendum-class, not book text.
- **The framing that changes the pitch:** IAFE's own text says fairs "may have rules and regulations that they impose on the local, county, state, provincial, and national levels," and that violators "shall be prohibited from future exhibitions per the rules adopted by the respective fairs and livestock shows." A Custer layer is not a deviation from the standard. It is the slot the standard leaves open. IAFE also binds "owners, exhibitors, fitters, trainers, and responsible persons," which is how the norm reaches parents without printing a parent-specific rule.
- **Reason for DISCUSS:** adoption binds the county to eight obligations, five of which are new as printed rules. Real weight, needs its own vote. Resolves audit Item 12.
- **Enforced-by:** licensed veterinarian (Items 2, 4, 5); Authority (Items 1, 3, 6, 7, 8); NALS&RMA Rules & Infraction Database via CSF's Barred Exhibitors provision.

## CC 2.8 — Penalties for Violations, and the perpetuity provision

- **Old Custer text:** no perpetuity provision exists anywhere in the county book (verified; all "prior" hits are false positives). The live mismatch: FB-DISCIPLINE-01's drafted auto-escalation reads "A third first-response sanction against the same participant in the same fair triggers severe-tier review" — a one-year memory against Matt's career-length direction of 8/9/2026.
- **Source finding:** CSF's model separates record permanence from consequence permanence. Its Barred Exhibitors provision reaches anyone "barred from any other show based on unethical practices, disqualified from competition at any major livestock show, from any state fair or county fair, or has had premiums withheld or withdrawn," with no time limit and national reach through the NALS&RMA database. And its Determination of Violations penalty list includes that an exhibitor "may be barred from competition at the State Fair for a determined period, including a lifetime suspension, upon the concurrence of the General Manager, the Director, and the Program Manager." Permanent exclusion IS within the norm, and it arrives with three-way concurrence attached. (A three-year ceiling appears only in the interference rule, not in the general violation structure — an earlier read that capped CSF at three years was corrected against source.)
- **New text:** adopts CSF GCR "Determination of Violations" verbatim with mandatory entity swaps — General Manager, Director, and Program Manager map to the Fair Board President, the CSU Extension Director or the FFA Advisor where the matter is FFA-specific, and the department Superintendent, per FB-DISCIPLINE-01's existing mapping. Penalties (a) through (f) adopted including cross-division clawback, removal from the sale, withheld sale proceeds with the three buyer options, and lifetime suspension on three-way concurrence. CSF's "Colorado State Fair Foundation" swaps to "the Custer County Fair" with no named recipient, per the volatility doctrine.
- **Custer Exceptions:** CC 2.8.1 — every warning and every penalty is permanent, recorded, and counts for as long as that person takes part in Custer County 4-H or FFA; no warning expires and no record is cleared. CC 2.8.2 — cites the CSF interference provision's "in any way, whether in person or by agent or representative," then applies it: these rules bind every person acting on behalf of, assisting, or accompanying an exhibitor, whether or not related — parents, guardians, siblings, grandparents, other relatives, friends, leaders, volunteers, fitters, trainers. A warning or penalty issued to any such person is recorded twice, against that person and against the exhibitor on whose behalf they were acting. CC 2.8.3 — warnings and penalties count across every project and every species. CC 2.8.4 — nothing is automatic; a prior record means the Authority already knows, it does not decide the outcome by itself.
- **Reason for DISCUSS:** perpetuity exists nowhere in the current book and reverses the drafted same-fair scope. The double-recording in 2.8.2 is what closes the gaming vector: route coaching through an uncle and the uncle carries a record that follows him to every family he helps, while the exhibitor's record moves anyway. No clean hand to hide behind.
- **WALKTHROUGH FLAG:** "An exhibitor is answerable for the conduct of the people they bring" is CSF's principle, not Custer's, but it will land hard the first time a twelve-year-old loses premiums because her uncle pounded on a rail. The board should say that out loud rather than discover it in a barn. Same flag class as disqualifying a member for a parent's conduct.
- **Enforced-by:** Authority, role-scoped. Ledger kept by the Fair Board secretary as a standing duty of the office — board policy, deliberately NOT printed in the book (see `notes.md`).
- **Cross-ref:** supersedes FB-DISCIPLINE-01's same-fair auto-escalation scope. Resolves audit Item 11.

## CC 2 — elevated general rules, racked from department text

- **Age eligibility.** Printed verbatim three times (FCS, market, non-market): "Exhibitors must be at least eight years of age and must not have reached their 19th birthday as of December 31 of the current enrollment year and must satisfy other eligibility requirements (if specified) for a class, to compete in that class." Rises to CC 2 once. The Cloverbud rule rides with it, printed twice with one word of drift ("at the State Fair" versus "at State Fair"): Cloverbud members under age 8 are not judged competitively, receive participation ribbons, may exhibit at the County Fair and other events but not at State Fair. Status: cleanup-class elevation, effect-neutral. Matt's direction: "That is fair rule. Not a project specific rule."
- **Judges' decisions are final.** Printed bare three times. CSF does NOT print this rule at all; what CSF prints is a grievance process scoped to violations and unethical activity, not to a judge's opinion. Custer's book contradicts itself: the Conduct section says decisions are final while the protest section says a protest "may be used by anyone to review rules, policies, and judging procedures." New structure: adopt CSF's grievance wording as the base, then a Custer Exception adds that a judge's placing is final and not protestable, while protest reaches rules, eligibility, and procedure. Status DISCUSS — resolving the contradiction in a direction changes effect. (The audit counted five printings; three are this rule. The others are the appeal panel's finality, a vet's call on a bitch in heat, and the Cat arbitration clause.)
- **Dress code.** Four versions: the full Colorado State 4-H code in Conduct (eight sub-bullets); FCS pointing to it; market's parenthetical (long sleeves, nice jeans, closed toe, no sandals, no hats); non-market repeating that and adding no gum or tobacco. Locked as TWO TIERS: a plain standard for being on the grounds, and a stricter standard applying any time an exhibitor is in front of a judge. Departments may print their own show dress deviations — Horse already carries a genuinely different ring standard ("Long sleeve button down shirts of any color, must have collar and cuffs. No 'show' shirts"), which stays local and is the proof case.
- **Fairgrounds cleanup.** Currently its own six-line section, never enumerated in the audit's unworked count. The obligation moves into CC 2 expectations; the dates ("Thursday, July 9, 2026 (mandatory) and immediately after the fair on Sunday, July 19, 2026") strip to the Addendum schedule as year-facts.
