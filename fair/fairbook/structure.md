# Fair Book Structure

The architecture of the revised Custer County Fair Book: how it is organized, how rules are numbered and cited, and how a rule is written. Doctrine for the mission lives in `charter.md`; this file is the book's own skeleton. Set 8/10/2026.

## Chapters

Every chapter's **section 1 is that chapter's shared layer**. CC 3.1 is what every market project obeys; CC 3.3 is what swine adds. A superintendent can be handed one numbered section and it is genuinely theirs.

Schedule and roster are gone from the book entirely; they are Addendum content. Fairgrounds cleanup stops being an orphan section and becomes an expectation in CC 2. Showmanship is consolidated into its own chapter rather than repeated inside each species chapter, because it spans species and Master Showmanship spans all of them at once.

Book order departs from the current county order, which places Horse and non-market between the market departments and the sale. Reader logic wins over legacy order, and the change is walkthrough-visible rather than silent (audit Item 16).

## Numbering

```
CC 3        chapter        MARKET LIVESTOCK
CC 3.3      section        Swine
CC 3.3.2    rule           the citable unit
CC 3.3.2(a) subpart        a condition inside that rule
      (i)   sub-subpart    rarely, only when (a) itself lists
```

The **CC prefix is load-bearing**. This book prints CSF rule references on nearly every page by design. Without a county tag, a parent cannot tell whose Rule 4.2 they are reading. Ours are visibly ours.

**Bullets are banned in rule text.** A bullet cannot be cited, so anything a family can be held to carries a number or a letter. Bullets survive only in non-binding material such as a what-to-bring box. If it is a bullet, it is not a rule, and everyone can tell at a glance.

## Citation

- **CSF cites by heading name.** The CSF General Competition Requirements carries no section numbers, no rule numbers, and no section symbol anywhere. It runs on named headings. So: "Custer County acknowledges and adopts the Colorado State Fair General Competition Requirements, Determination of Violations." A parent can open the state's document, find that heading, and confirm the words match. Verified against source 8/10/2026.
- **CSF abbreviates to "CSF"** in the book, defined at first use in CC 1.
- **IAFE cites by item number.** Its eight guidelines are numbered, so "IAFE National Code of Show Ring Ethics, Item 7" is a real citation.

## How a section is built

Every department section is a **complete map**, not a list of leftovers:

1. An **applies-to-you index** naming every rule that reaches that project, by number and title, in about one screen.
2. The section's own **deviations and additions**, printed in full.
3. **Consequence-bearing rules reprinted in full**, in both places — weigh-in deadlines, health inspection, anything that gets a kid disqualified or an animal hurt. Identical words in both locations, generated from the same source so they cannot drift.

The third item is deliberate redundancy and it is a safety feature. A kid in a barn at six in the morning should never have to page backward a chapter to find out he missed a deadline.

## How a rule is written

Official-sounding and loophole-resistant pull in the opposite direction from complicated. Loopholes do not live in simple sentences. They live in vocabulary drift — this book's own "termination" problem, the same word meaning show-ineligibility in one place and slaughter in another, is the proof.

- Short sentences, active voice, a named actor. "The exhibitor must weigh in on Thursday," never "weigh-in shall be accomplished."
- **Must / must not** is mandatory. **May** is permitted. **Will** is the fair promising something. "Should" never appears.
- Every weight-bearing term is defined once in CC 1 and used identically forever. No synonyms, ever.
- Numerals for anything countable, so a kid can scan for the number.
- A rule with a consequence prints the consequence. A rule with a check prints who does the checking.
- Rule text targets a fifth-grade reading level.

What makes the book feel official is the citation format, the defined terms, and consistent typography — not the sentences. A parent who wants to argue is stopped by a rule that says exactly one thing, not by a rule that says it in six clauses.

## Two registers

Adopted text stays in its source's voice, formal, because it should sound like the source it came from. Custer exceptions are written plain, because they are the county talking directly to a family about what the rule means for them. A reader can tell at a glance which is which, and learns fast that the plain paragraphs are the ones telling him what to actually do.

The pattern is explained once in CC 1.

Audience is eight through eighteen, with enough weight that the rules sound official — because it is the parents who hunt loopholes.

## The examples pattern

State the explicit adopted rule, then print the county's current specifics beneath it as examples. The examples illustrate the rule; they never limit it.

This solves the flavor problem structurally. The veterans' own language survives verbatim as illustration while the rule above it is the standard. Nothing gets thrown out and nothing drifts. First applied at CC 2.7, where eight verbatim printings of the coaching rule become examples of an adopted interference standard.

## Citation-first exceptions

Any Custer exception that extends, narrows, or applies an adopted rule **cites the source first**, then states the county's application. Exceptions that are purely local carry no citation, because there is nothing to cite.

Without the citation, an exception carrying a CSF principle reads as Custer inventing liability. With it, the county is stating how an existing standard applies here.

## The racking standard

Every rule that prints more than once is found and racked up to the shared layer at the top of its chapter. Some rules still repeat downward into departments, and that is correct — but a repeat is then **deliberate**, and it prints in identical words and identical formatting everywhere it appears.

Merge is no longer the automatic answer. Rack, then repeat on purpose.

## Pass sequencing

The shared layer is built first, complete, so departments write against a finished skeleton rather than being re-touched after the fact. Item 6 and Item 7 of the 8/10 audit already carry verified receipts for what belongs up top. A short reconciliation sweep runs at the end to catch repeats the department passes surface that the audit did not.
