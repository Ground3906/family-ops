# Working Group Change Log

Matt's carry copy. The working group holds the Version 1.0 book built 8/17/2026. This file lists every change locked after that build, keyed to **their** citation numbers so an item raised in the meeting can be found by the number in front of them.

**Scope.** This log is true only against the version the working group currently holds. When they adopt or reject, this file retires and a fresh log opens against the next version they hold. Do not carry rows forward.

**How to use it in the meeting.** They raise an issue, find the row by their citation, agree, read the proposed change.

## Changes

| Their citation | What it says now | Proposed change | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| CC 1.8.1 | The Authority is six roles: Fair Board, superintendent, judge, licensed veterinarian, Extension agent, FFA advisor. | Add (f), the sale committee, in the conduct of the Market Livestock Sale, including sale order, the exit election, and the handling of sale proceeds and required disclosures. | The sale committee acts in four places in CC 4 and is defined nowhere. It makes calls in the flow of the sale the way a superintendent does in a department. |
| CC 1 (definitions) | Neither custody nor care is defined. CC 2.15 uses both words interchangeably. | Add Custody: legal control of an animal, including authority to move, hold, release, or deliver it. Add Care: the daily work of feeding, watering, bedding, and tending condition. Care is not custody. | The terminal sale splits the two for the first time. Without definitions the split cannot be stated cleanly. |
| CC 2.3 | Discipline may be imposed by any member of the Fair Board, any 4-H leader, the FFA advisor, any superintendent, or any CSU Extension personnel with oversight responsibility at the fair. The rest of the rule then says "the Authority." | Discipline may be imposed by the Authority. A person who is not the Authority reports prohibited behavior to the Authority. | The rule defines its actors by list and then uses the defined term for the same actors. Narrows sanctioning power to CC 1.8.1 roles and gives 4-H leaders a stated path. |
| CC 2.12 | An exhibitor releases the Fair Board and Custer County. | An exhibitor releases the Fair Board, Custer County, and each person acting as the Authority under CC 1.8.1. | Superintendents, judges, and the veterinarian act under this book and are not covered by the release as written. Worth a county attorney read before print. |
| CC 3.1.14 | The Fair Board may declare the show of that species a terminal show. | The Authority may declare it. | Disease call made in the moment by whoever is competent, with the veterinarian in role. |
| CC 3.1.15 | Rules Stated Elsewhere sits fourteen rules deep. | Moves to CC 3.1.1. | The index rule opens every other unit in the book. Burying it in the most-read subchapter defeats it. |
| CC 4.1.1 | Titled "The Sale." States the sale is terminal. Says nothing about what the buyer gets. | Title becomes "The Terminal Sale." Folds in: the purchase conveys the right to the processed product only, never possession of a live animal, and no sale animal is returned, gifted, resold, or released alive. | The governing fact of the chapter carried no signal in the heading or the table of contents. The buyer's question and the exhibitor's question are the same fact. |
| CC 4.2 | The sale order is determined by the sale committee or the Fair Board. | The sale order is determined by the sale committee. | "A or B" means nobody in particular decides, and both sides have a rule to point at when order is contested. |
| CC 4.4.1 | Exhibitor responsible for care, feeding, and daily custody through the night before delivery. | Custody stays with the exhibitor until the animal enters the sale ring, then passes to the Authority until the animal is loaded on the processing facility's truck. Exhibitor care runs through close of barn on sale day. | Matches what actually happens. Separates care from custody so the exhibitor is not on the hook for control of an animal they no longer control. |
| CC 4.4.2 | The Fair Board delivers every sale animal to the designated slaughter facility. Care and custody end when the animal is loaded. | The Authority delivers every consigned animal to the truck of the processing facility. The exhibitor is not part of the delivery. | The fair does not haul to the plant. The processor's truck comes. Care end moved to CC 4.4.1 where custody is stated. |
| CC 4.4.4 | The sale is terminal, every animal goes to slaughter at a Fair Board designated facility, purchase conveys product only, no live release. | Struck. | Its possession content moves to CC 4.1.1 and the designating party is stated in full at CC 4.4.5. A rule that only echoes two others is where contradictions start. |
| CC 4.4.5, CC 4.4.6 | The Fair Board designates facilities, may designate USDA and custom-exempt facilities, and may arrange a post-sale rest period. | The Authority, three instances. | These happen in the flow of the sale by whoever is competent. |
| CC 5.12 | Rules That Reach This Department sits eleven rules deep. | Moves to CC 5.1. | Same as CC 3.1.15. A horse exhibitor reads the whole department before learning which general rules reach them. |
| CC 7.1, CC 8, CC 9.12, CC 10.1.14 | CC 7.1 and CC 8 carry no index rule. CC 9.12 and CC 10.1.14 name the Fair Board. | New index rule opens CC 7.1 and CC 8, and both chapter openers drop the now-duplicated CC 2 sentence. CC 9.12 becomes the Authority. CC 10.1.14 non-responsibility clause matches CC 2.12's construction. | These two chapters are the most likely to be read as self-contained, which is the failure the index rule prevents. Two liability clauses in one book should not use different constructions. |
