Currently there are no assigned handicap parking spots at either fairgrounds entrance. An attendee in a wheelchair had to make do without a designated space this year, which prompted this item. Beef Chute: Market Sale Day Safety Capital Safety

During the 2026 market sale, spectators including kids and parents were entering the beef chute area, which created a real safety concern and required active management on the day. Worth bringing to the board to discuss how to better address this going forward.

Two areas to think through together:

1. Physical barrier: Some form of railing, panel fencing, or secured barrier along the chute would go a long way toward keeping folks out of the area without needing someone stationed there the whole time.

2. Announcements: It would also help to have a chute safety reminder built into the announcer's script, ideally before the sale opens and again at a natural break during the event. Happy to help draft language if that would be useful.

Operational Open Items

Sheep Barn Setup: Opportunities to Improve for 2027 Operational Safety / Welfare

The 2026 sheep barn setup ran into a few challenges that are worth discussing as a group, with an eye toward doing things differently next year:

Electrical: With outlets only on the exterior walls, extension cords ended up running across animal cages to reach interior areas. Beyond being untidy, it created tripping concerns and wasn't ideal from an animal welfare standpoint either.

Space: The pen arrangement left a fair amount of unused space. There's likely a better way to lay out the footprint that gets more utility out of the barn.

Heat / Holding Pens: By mutual agreement between superintendents, swine holding pens were relocated outside during the fair. The intent was to solve a space problem, and the decision was made in good faith. In practice, outdoor conditions were too warm to be a workable solution for the animals. This is a planning and setup issue, not a personnel one, the right answer is a barn layout that doesn't put superintendents in a position where that trade-off has to be made at all.

A fresh look at the overall layout before next year seems worthwhile. Flagging for board awareness. The actual layout work would be done separately and brought back to the group.

Info Board Location: Sale Day Operational

This past year the info board was positioned at the beef chute, and it drew a lot of public foot traffic right into that area. Combined with the chute safety concerns noted above, it created some congestion in a spot that really needed to stay clear.

One idea worth considering: splitting into two boards serving two different audiences.

1. Public-facing board: Somewhere visible and accessible to general attendees, away from animal movement areas. Location would be up to the group to decide.

2. Participant board: Inside the sheep barn for exhibitors and families who need the working details, class order, times, pen assignments, and so on.

New Fairgrounds: A Painted View Ranch Strategic

A property worth being aware of: 159.8 acres at 3115 County Road 160, Westcliffe, currently listed at $7.25M. A briefing document has been put together that outlines a possible funding approach, including Congressionally Directed Spending (Senators Bennet and Hickenlooper, Rep. Pettersen CO-7), GOCO Land Acquisition, DOLA EIAF, USDA Rural Development Community Facilities, conservation easement through San Isabel Land Protection Trust, Wet Mountain Valley Community Foundation, the Conservation Trust Fund lottery, and a bridge financing structure to handle the timing gap between a live listing and grant cycles.

The intent is to frame this as an opportunity for the board and commissioners to consider, not a funding problem they're being handed. If there's interest, a fuller conversation with the BOCC could be worth exploring. November 2027 is likely a more realistic window than 2026 for any ballot measure, if that path is pursued.
